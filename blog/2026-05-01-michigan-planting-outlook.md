Spring 2026 is shaping up to be a tale of two halves across Michigan's Corn Belt. Northern Thumb counties are tracking 80–120 GDD behind the 10-year average heading into May, while the southern tier is running close to normal.

**What this means for planting:**

- In the northern Thumb (Huron, Sanilac, Tuscola), soil temps at 2″ are still hovering around 46–48°F. Wait for a sustained 50°F reading before dropping corn seed.
- Southern Michigan (Lenawee, Monroe, Hillsdale) hit the 50°F threshold in late April. If you haven't planted yet, the next dry window looks favorable.
- Soybean growers everywhere should monitor for Phytophthora risk—wet April soils plus cool temps are textbook conditions for damping-off.
