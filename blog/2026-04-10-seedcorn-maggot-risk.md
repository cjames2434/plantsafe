Seedcorn maggot (*Delia platura*) is one of the most underestimated early-season pests in the Corn Belt. Adults emerge when soil GDD (base 39°F) accumulates past ~200, and they're attracted to freshly disturbed soil with decaying organic matter.

**High-risk conditions:**

- Recently applied manure (especially fresh, unincorporated)
- Heavy crop residue from a previous cover crop
- No-till fields with green-bridge vegetation
- Cool, wet springs that slow seedling emergence (longer exposure window)

PlantSafe integrates ISU's pheromone-trap biofix network with a location-specific GDD accumulation model to estimate your field's maggot pressure for each planting day in the 14-day forecast window.

If you're in a high-risk field, consider a neonicotinoid seed treatment or delay planting until the first adult flight peaks and declines (usually around 450 GDD base 39°F).
