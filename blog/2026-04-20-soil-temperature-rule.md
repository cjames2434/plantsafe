title: "When to Plant Corn in Michigan: The Soil Temperature Rule"
---

The single best predictor of corn emergence success is consistent soil temperature at seeding depth. University research across the Corn Belt converges on the same threshold: **wait for 50°F at 2″ depth, sustained for 3+ days**.

Why does this matter so much?

- **Imbibitional chilling:** Corn seed absorbs water rapidly in the first 24–48 hours. If soil temps dip below 50°F during this window, cell membranes can rupture—permanently reducing stand.
- **Pythium risk:** Cold, wet soils favor Pythium damping-off. Seed treatments help, but they don't replace warm soil.
- **Uneven emergence:** A 48-hour cold snap after planting can stagger emergence by 1–2 leaf stages—costing yield at every plant that comes up late.
